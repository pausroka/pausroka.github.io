Case study
Garmat USA · 2024

Making manufacturing configurable

Turning a phone-and-spreadsheet sales process for industrial spray booths into a guided, self-service configurator with real-time pricing.

IndustryIndustrial manufacturing · B2B
UsersDistributors
FocusConfigurator · quoting · design system
01 · Challenge

Thousands of combinations, ordered by phone

Garmat USA manufactures industrial spray booths: highly specialized equipment with thousands of possible configuration combinations.

Before the project, ordering relied almost entirely on phone calls, emails and spreadsheets. Distributors contacted the sales team, specifications were reviewed manually, and quotes could take several days to prepare. Compatibility rules, pricing logic and manufacturing requirements lived in people's heads, which created delays and increased the risk of error.

The challenge was not simply building a configurator. It was translating a complex sales and manufacturing process into an intuitive digital experience.

Before
  • Phone calls, emails and spreadsheets
  • Specifications checked by hand
  • Quotes in several days
  • Depends on expert knowledge
After
  • Self-service, one decision at a time
  • Only valid options are shown
  • Price updates as you build
  • Complete specification in one session
02 · Role

From expert knowledge to a guided journey

I led UX/UI design, built the design system and owned the end-to-end configuration experience. The project meant defining workflows that didn't exist before, and turning expert knowledge into a journey any distributor can follow.

03 · Configuration

One decision at a time

The first step was understanding how products were configured internally, and which decisions distributors actually needed to make. Instead of presenting every option at once, I designed a structured flow that guides users through configuration step by step.

The configuration flowChoice → priced specification
  1. 01

    Booth model

    Pick the base model.

  2. 02

    Cabin

    Length and height, with custom options.

  3. 03

    Upgrades

    Roof, drive-thru and more.

  4. 04

    Equipment

    Lighting and mechanical options.

  5. 05

    Summary

    Full specification and price, ready to send.

Users only see options relevant to their previous selections. What was a highly technical product became a predictable, guided experience.

04 · Complexity

Making product logic invisible

Behind the scenes, every booth carries dependencies, restrictions and technical requirements. Rather than exposing these rules, the interface quietly steers users toward valid configurations.

The result is a system distributors can use confidently, without needing expert knowledge of manufacturing constraints.

Screen 01

The configurator

Two columns with clear roles. On the right, the distributor works through grouped decisions: booth model, cabin, upgrades, mechanicals and equipment. On the left, a summary lists every part already selected, with its code and quantity, next to a picture of the booth. A check mark confirms each completed step, and the bar at the bottom keeps the next action in reach: add to request or proceed.

Screen 02

Warnings that explain, not block

Some choices are allowed but have consequences, like raising the cabin height. The option gets a yellow outline and a short message explaining what changes. In the summary, the same item is highlighted and marked with a coloured dot, and every group shows how many of its items are complete, so nothing important slips through before the request is sent.

Screen 03

Errors that stop invalid booths

When two choices can't work together, the conflicting options are outlined in red with an explanation, and the incompatible alternatives are disabled. Add to request and Proceed stay inactive until the conflict is solved, so an invalid configuration never reaches production. A Show only alerts switch filters the long list down to what needs attention.

05 · System

A design system built to grow

Alongside the configurator, I built a reusable UI system in cooperation with the US team, so every Garmat product stays consistent across different platforms and needs. It covers tokens for color, typography, sizes and shadows, components and page templates. New configuration steps, products and workflows can be added later without breaking the experience or rebuilding from scratch.

A file the whole team can work in

File organisation

The file opens with an index sheet. Every page follows the portal itself, from log in and the product catalog to requests, orders and checkout, so anyone on the team can find a flow in seconds.

One shared library

The design system lives in its own file, split into tokens, templates and patterns, media and components. The portal uses it as a library, so a change made once reaches every screen.

Short update sessions

Regular short sessions with the team to walk through changes, agree on updates and keep everyone working from the same version.

Constant cooperation

Close, ongoing work with the PM and especially with frontend, so components in Figma and in code stay in step.

06 · Feedback

Pricing as part of the configuration

No more waiting days for a quote that should take seconds.

Pricing became part of the configuration itself, not a separate step handled later by sales. Users get immediate feedback as they build their product, so they understand the impact of each decision without waiting on manual calculations or follow-up emails. That shift increased transparency and removed much of the uncertainty buyers used to feel.

07 · Result

From days of back-and-forth to one session

The final product replaced a fragmented, manual workflow with a structured self-service experience. Distributors can now configure products independently, get immediate feedback and submit complete specifications without the lengthy back-and-forth that used to define the process.

What was once a knowledge-dependent workflow became a scalable digital system: less friction, better accuracy, and a better experience for customers and internal teams alike.
